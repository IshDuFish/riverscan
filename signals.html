How to read a signal

Anatomy of a signal

TICKERDate slipDate spotted

What changed, in one sentence, with the old and new values.

  • Ticker — the London-listed company behind the trial's sponsor or the contract's supplier.
  • Type — what kind of change it is (below).
  • Severity — how much this kind of change usually matters (below).
  • Record ID — links straight to the original trial record or contract notice, so you can check it yourself.

Signal types

Status change

A trial's status moved — for example from Recruiting to Active, Suspended, Terminated, Withdrawn or Completed. Terminations and suspensions are the changes people most often want to know about.

Date slip

The estimated primary completion date moved. A later date means the trial is expected to take longer than the sponsor last said. Delays can happen for many reasons, from slow recruitment to adding new trial sites, so one slip on its own rarely tells the whole story. Repeated slips are more notable.

Enrolment

The target or actual number of participants changed. A large cut can mean a trial is being scaled back; an increase can mean it's being expanded.

Phase change

A programme moved stage — for example from dose escalation to dose expansion, or from Phase 1 to Phase 2.

Results posted

The sponsor has uploaded results to the registry. Worth reading against the company's own announcement.

New trial

A new trial has been registered under the company. New trials often appear on the registry before they are announced.

Halted Safety measure

The EU register records temporary halts, urgent safety measures and serious breaches. These are rare and always rated high.

Registry mismatch

The same trial is often registered in more than one place. We link the records and flag it when one registry says the trial is still running while another says it's over. Often one record just hasn't been updated yet. Sometimes it's the first place a change shows up.

Government contract signals

Contract award

A UK public body has awarded work to a company we cover. The signal shows the buyer, the value and a link to the official notice on Find a Tender or Contracts Finder.

Framework place

The company has been named as one of several approved suppliers on a framework or purchasing system. The headline value is usually shared between suppliers and may never be fully spent, so framework places are rated lower than solo contract wins.

Severity

  • High — a trial is terminated, withdrawn, suspended or halted, results are posted, a programme changes phase, registries disagree about a stopped trial, or a contract worth £100m or more is won.
  • Medium — a trial completes, a date moves, enrolment changes by 20% or more, a new trial is registered, registries disagree, a contract worth £5m or more is won, or a framework place worth £100m or more is announced.
  • Low — smaller housekeeping changes.

A worked example

In February 2026, Synairgen announced it was ending its Phase 2 trial of SNG001 because recruitment was far behind expectations. Afterwards, the EU registry (CTIS) recorded the trial as Ended, while the UK registry (ISRCTN) recorded it as Completed. Small differences like that are common. RiverScan flags the bigger kind: one registry still saying a trial is running after another says it's over.

What a signal is not. A signal tells you that something changed. It does not tell you whether a company is a good or bad investment, and it is never a recommendation to buy or sell. Always read the original record and the company's own announcements. See our terms and disclaimer.
