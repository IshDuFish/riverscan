RIVERSCAN WEEKLY | 26 November 2026

Four signals this week, across four companies. Cohort won a £8.4m contract from Defence Equipment and Support; two registries disagree about one of Faron Pharmaceuticals' trials; Galliford Try was named on a £1.2bn YPO framework; and Tracsis won a £20m contract from Network Rail Infrastructure Limited. Another 17 smaller changes are in the full log.

CONTRACTS
---------

Cohort (CHRT)
Defence Equipment and Support awarded Cohort a £8.4m contract for Joint Command and Staff Training (JCAST). That's roughly 2.8% of its £306.4m annual revenue.
Contract award, medium importance. Source: Find a Tender, https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/112028-2026

Galliford Try (GFRD)
YPO named Galliford Try as a supplier on a £1.2bn framework for Collaborative Partner Organisation £5m+. Frameworks are shared and often only partly used, so Galliford Try's slice isn't known. For scale, its annual revenue is £1.9bn.
Framework place, medium importance. Source: Find a Tender, https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/107838-2026

Tracsis (TRCS)
Network Rail Infrastructure Limited awarded Tracsis a £20m contract for Safety-Critical Workforce Planning and Line Blockage System, running 7.8 years. That works out at £2.6m a year, roughly 3.9% of its £65.2m annual revenue.
Contract award, medium importance. Source: Find a Tender, https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/111877-2026

CLINICAL TRIALS
---------------

Faron Pharmaceuticals (FARN)
The same Faron Pharmaceuticals trial (“A Study to Assess Safety, Tolerability and Preliminary Efficacy of Bexmarilimab in…”) is registered in two places, and they no longer agree: ClinicalTrials.gov still lists it as active, not recruiting, while EU CTIS shows it as ended. Usually one record just hasn't been updated yet, but a gap like this can be the first public sign that a trial has wound down.
Registry mismatch, medium importance. Source: ClinicalTrials.gov vs EU CTIS, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05428969
